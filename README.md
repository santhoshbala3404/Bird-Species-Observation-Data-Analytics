# Bird Species Observation Analysis

## Project Overview

This project focuses on analyzing bird species observation data to understand species distribution, habitat preferences, seasonal trends, and observer contributions.

The analysis combines forest and grassland observation datasets and explores bird sightings across different locations, habitats, months, and environmental conditions.

The objective is to identify frequently observed species and understand how bird observations vary across different habitats and time periods.

## Tools & Technologies
- **Python:** Data analysis and preprocessing.
- **Pandas & NumPy:** Data cleaning and manipulation.
- **Matplotlib & Seaborn:** Exploratory data visualization.
- **Plotly:** Interactive visualizations.
- **Power BI:** Interactive dashboard analysis.

## Key Areas of Analysis
- Overall bird species observation counts.
- Species distribution across different habitats.
- Monthly and seasonal observation trends.
- Observer contribution analysis.
- Bird observations across different sites and plots.
- Environmental factors such as temperature, humidity, sky conditions, and wind.
- Species-level observation patterns.

## Key Insights
- Northern Cardinal is the most frequently observed species overall, with more than 600 observations.
- Red-eyed Vireo is the most frequently observed species in forest habitats.
- Field Sparrow is the most frequently observed species in grassland habitats.
- June records the highest number of bird observations.
- A relatively small number of observers contribute a significant share of the recorded observations.

## Project Files
- `bird_species_observation_eda.ipynb` – Python exploratory data analysis.
- `birds_observed_cleaned data.csv` – Cleaned bird observation dataset.
- `bird_species_power_bi_project.pbix` – Power BI dashboard.
- `Bird Species Observation Analysis.pdf` – Detailed analysis report.

## Conclusion

This project demonstrates how data analytics can support biodiversity monitoring by identifying species distribution patterns, habitat preferences, and seasonal observation trends. These insights can help inform conservation planning and improve wildlife data collection efforts.
